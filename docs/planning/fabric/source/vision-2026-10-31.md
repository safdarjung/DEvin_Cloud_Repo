# Fabric: complete product vision and feature inventory

Source: owner's consolidated vision, pasted 2026-10-31. Verbatim content, light markdown formatting only. This is the canonical statement of intent; `01-master-prd.md` normalizes it into feature ids and `03-roadmap-and-tracker.md` tracks delivery. Edit this file only to record a new owner decision, with a dated note.

> The most important distinction is that Fabric is the overall platform and company; CoolSkool is its first industry-specific product for education. The school ERP is the starting point, not the limit of the company.

**FABRIC — a universal adaptive intelligence platform.** One shared intelligence foundation. Many industry-specific applications. Personal AI for individuals. Secure automation and continuous improvement.

Planning counts (conceptual, not claims about completed software): 5 major product layers · 8+ long-term product domains · 14 conceptual CoolSkool workspaces · 1 shared intelligence foundation.

## 1. The overall Fabric platform

### 1.1 Universal intelligence core
The shared foundation that all Fabric products use:
- A unified identity and account system.
- Individual users, organisations, institutions, and households.
- Multiple organisations and workspaces under appropriate accounts.
- User profiles, preferences, roles, and permissions.
- Organisation-specific settings, rules, goals, and context.
- A reusable AI and agent infrastructure.
- Shared knowledge, search, retrieval, and memory services.
- Data connectors and integration infrastructure.
- Workflow and automation infrastructure.
- Analytics, forecasting, recommendations, and simulation interfaces.
- Notifications and activity history.
- Module and feature management.
- Audit trails, observability, security, and administration.
- Subscription, entitlements, and billing foundations.
- Reusable interfaces and APIs for future products.

### 1.2 Multi-industry architecture
Fabric must not be permanently tied to education. The same core should be capable of supporting industry-specific applications, with independent modules and business rules.

| Product domain | Long-term intention |
|---|---|
| Education | CoolSkool: school operations and educational intelligence |
| Personal AI | An independent AI assistant and personal intelligence workspace |
| Households | Household organisation, planning, documents and coordination |
| Small businesses | Business operations, analytics and automation |
| Healthcare | Clinic and healthcare administration, with appropriate clinical safeguards |
| Professional services | Client, project, workflow and knowledge management |
| Finance | Financial analysis, portfolio intelligence and risk management |
| Other institutions | Custom applications built on Fabric's shared core |

Each industry should have its own domain pack, data models, workflows, integrations, permissions, and evaluation standards.

### 1.3 Modular architecture
- Shared Fabric Core.
- Independently versioned industry modules.
- Customer-specific configurations.
- Custom modules.
- Reusable integrations.
- Module dependencies and compatibility checks.
- Module installation, activation, disabling, upgrading, and rollback.
- Customer-specific permissions and entitlements.
- A central marketplace for discovering capabilities.

The objective is to reuse the platform without forcing every industry to use the same complicated interface or data structure.

## 2. Identity, accounts, workspaces and permissions

### 2.1 Universal identity
Individual user accounts · Organisation accounts · Institution accounts · Household accounts · Multiple memberships · Role-based access · Fine-grained permission controls · Role and membership changes · Invitation and onboarding flows · Account recovery and session management.

### 2.2 Personal and institutional separation
A person may use Fabric through a school, employer, or other organisation, but should also be able to have an independent personal Fabric account. The platform must support: personal workspace · institutional or business workspace · personal AI memory · institutional context and memory · separate documents and files · separate connections and integrations · separate agents and workflows · separate data ownership and retention rules · explicit permission to move or share information between workspaces · workspace switching · account portability · independent personal subscriptions.

### 2.3 Leaving an institution
When a student, teacher, employee, or other user leaves an organisation: institutional access is revoked appropriately · personal account access remains independent · eligible personal settings and personal information remain available · organisation-owned records remain protected · confidential records are not copied into the personal workspace · personal information is not silently transferred to the institution · institutional integrations and credentials are disconnected as appropriate · applicable retention and legal requirements are followed.

This must be implemented as a real data and authorisation boundary — not merely a switch between two dashboard screens.

### 2.4 Permission system
Roles and permissions · resource-level access · data-level access · action-level permissions · temporary access · delegated access · approval permissions · agent-specific permissions · integration-specific scopes · permission reviews · access revocation · audit history.

## 3. Fabric AI and intelligence infrastructure

### 3.1 Multi-model intelligence
Multiple AI providers · multiple model families · provider-independent model abstraction · model routing · task-specific model selection · model capability registry · context and token management · cost tracking · latency and reliability monitoring · fallback models · evaluation-based model selection · model versioning · model performance comparisons.

### 3.2 Hybrid local and cloud AI
**On-device AI:** lightweight local models · local inference · private, device-side tasks · local context retrieval · offline support for compatible tasks · reduced cloud dependency · low-latency assistance.
**Cloud AI:** complex reasoning · longer context · more demanding analytical tasks · specialist model capabilities · workloads that exceed device capabilities.
**Intelligent model routing:** decide whether a task can run locally · decide when cloud execution is necessary · consider privacy, consent, cost, latency, device capabilities, and model quality · avoid sending restricted information to external providers · support provider changes without rewriting product functionality.

Google AI Edge and LiteRT-LM were discussed as possible technical options for on-device inference. They are candidates to evaluate, not a commitment.

### 3.3 Universal AI interface
Natural-language input · context-aware conversations · file and document interaction · search across authorised records · structured answers · recommendations · action suggestions · tool invocation · agent handoff · workflow initiation · conversation history · context-aware follow-up · user feedback. The AI should help people complete real tasks, not just answer questions.

## 4. Memory and personalisation

### 4.1 Personal memory
User preferences · goals and priorities · important facts the user chooses to retain · personal working context · relevant history · long-term projects · personal knowledge retrieval · memory inspection · memory correction · memory deletion · memory export · retention settings · permission-controlled memory access.

### 4.2 Institutional memory
Organisation knowledge · policies and procedures · historical records · operational context · department knowledge · institutional objectives · important workflows · approved documents · decision history.

### 4.3 Learning and adaptation
Three separate mechanisms, implemented only where appropriate: (1) personal contextual memory; (2) local adaptation (lightweight adaptation or LoRA where practical); (3) shared model improvement (opt-in federated learning, differential privacy, secure aggregation). Private conversations must not automatically become shared training material. Federated learning must not be presented as an automatic privacy guarantee. Each mechanism needs explicit consent, data controls, evaluation, and governance.

## 5. Agents, automation and workflow workforce

### 5.1 AI agents
Specialist agents · agent roles and descriptions · agent-specific tools · permissions and access scopes · agent instructions and configuration · model selection · agent memory where authorised · agent activity history · agent task status · human handoff · performance evaluations.

### 5.2 Workflow automation
Visual workflow creation · trigger configuration · scheduled execution · event-based execution · conditional branches · approval gates · tool execution · retry and timeout rules · failure handling · notifications · workflow logs · pause, resume, and disable · workflow versioning.

### 5.3 Agent workforce management
Agent directory · availability · task queue · task assignment · task dependencies · parallel work · execution monitoring · agent-to-agent coordination · cost monitoring · error tracking · quality scores · approval requirements.

### 5.4 Safe autonomous actions
Actions classified by risk: read-only · low-risk reversible · user-confirmed · human-approved high-impact · prohibited. An agent must not receive unrestricted authority simply because it can perform a task. All important agent actions must be permission-checked and auditable.

## 7. Intelligence, insights, forecasting and recommendations

Move beyond reporting what happened to what is happening, what could happen, and what to do about it.

- **7.1 Descriptive:** live summaries · dashboards · historical trends · period comparisons · department/team performance · resource utilisation · exception detection · data-quality warnings · KPIs · drill-down to records.
- **7.2 Diagnostic:** unusual patterns · possible reasons · contributing factors · recurring problems · bottlenecks · supporting data · evidence vs hypotheses · missing or unreliable information.
- **7.3 Predictive:** demand, workload, capacity, resource, financial forecasting · risk prediction · trend projection · early-warning signals · confidence and uncertainty · forecast accuracy measurement.
- **7.4 Prescriptive:** recommend actions · explain why · estimate benefits and costs · compare options · consider constraints · identify responsible people · suggest next steps · track whether acted upon · measure whether the outcome improved.
- **7.5 What-if simulation:** questions like "What happens if I change the timetable / increase or decrease staff / enrolment increases / costs rise / change a process / choose a different financial strategy?" The simulator supports adjustable assumptions · multiple scenarios · baseline and scenario comparison · projected outcomes · resource and cost implications · risks and constraints · sensitivity analysis · confidence ranges · save and revisit · human approval before implementation. A scenario based on assumptions is not proof that an intervention will produce the predicted outcome.
- **7.6 Continuous outcome measurement:** track decisions · record rationale, expected result, actual result · compare · evaluate forecast accuracy and recommendation usefulness · identify ineffective workflows · improve future recommendations through measured evidence.

## 8. Digital twin of a person or organisation

- **8.1 Institutional twin:** students, teachers, departments, classrooms, buildings, timetables, facilities, resources, activities, financial resources, workflows, capacity, historical performance, operational dependencies — connected so a change in one area can be evaluated against the rest.
- **8.2 Personal twin:** goals, schedule, tasks, preferences, resources, voluntarily supplied habits, projects, commitments, knowledge, progress. User-controlled and privacy-aware; never an undisclosed profiling system.
- **8.3 Interface:** visual representation · connected entities and relationships · resource and capacity maps · activity and dependency views · historical, current and scenario state · forecast overlays · change-impact analysis · drill-down to evidence.
- **8.4 Simulation:** change one variable · model related effects · compare scenarios · detect resource conflicts · identify constraints · estimate consequences · show uncertainty · save scenario versions · compare simulated and actual results. Not a colourful diagram: it needs reliable underlying data and a meaningful relationship to the real organisation.

## 23. What must never be confused with an implemented feature

| Concept | What it really means |
|---|---|
| AI insight | A generated explanation supported by relevant data |
| Predictive model | A model with measured predictive performance |
| Digital twin | A structured model connected to real, appropriately updated information |
| What-if simulator | An explicit scenario model with defined assumptions |
| Autonomous agent | A tool-using system operating within enforceable permissions |
| Personal memory | User-controlled information that can be inspected and managed |
| Self-improving AI | A measured, controlled improvement process, not an unrestricted self-modifying system |
| Marketplace installation | A module that actually installs, configures and passes compatibility checks |
| Custom Studio delivery | Software built, tested, reviewed and deployed through controlled procedures |

A dashboard can demonstrate all these concepts; the real product requires the underlying data, logic, infrastructure, integrations, safeguards and evaluation.

## The ultimate vision
**Fabric. One intelligence foundation. Every person. Every institution. Every possibility.**

A school uses CoolSkool to manage students, teachers, fees, academics and operations. Fabric understands the school's data, identifies problems, explains causes, forecasts outcomes, simulates alternatives, recommends improvements and helps authorised people carry them out. The school installs modules or describes a need and has Fabric help create a solution. Each teacher, parent and student can also have an appropriately separate personal AI experience. The same foundation later powers a business, a household, a clinic or a financial organisation. Not simply a school ERP, an AI assistant, or an agent platform: a shared intelligence and software platform that helps people and organisations understand, operate and improve their world.
