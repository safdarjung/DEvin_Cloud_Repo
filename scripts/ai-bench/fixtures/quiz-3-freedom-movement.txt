Unit: The Indian Freedom Movement, 1857–1947 (Grade VIII Social Science)

The struggle for India's independence from British colonial rule spanned nearly a century and involved many strategies, from armed uprisings to organized non-violent resistance. The Revolt of 1857, sometimes called the First War of Independence, was an early large-scale rebellion against the East India Company's rule, though it was eventually suppressed. In 1885, the Indian National Congress was founded to give Indians a platform to voice political demands within the British system. Over the early twentieth century, the movement grew more assertive, with the Partition of Bengal in 1905 sparking the Swadeshi movement, which encouraged the boycott of British goods in favour of Indian-made products. From the 1920s onward, non-violent civil disobedience became the dominant strategy, including the Non-Cooperation Movement, the Salt March of 1930, and the Quit India Movement of 1942, which called for an immediate end to British rule. Alongside these mass movements, revolutionary groups and the Indian National Army pursued more direct resistance. Religious and political tensions between communities also shaped the final years of the struggle, eventually contributing to the partition of the subcontinent into India and Pakistan when independence was finally achieved on 15 August 1947. Students should be able to sequence these major events on a timeline and explain the shift from constitutional methods to mass civil disobedience.
