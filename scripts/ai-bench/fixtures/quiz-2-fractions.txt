Unit: Fractions — Addition and Subtraction (Grade V Mathematics)

A fraction represents a part of a whole and is written as a numerator over a denominator, such as 3/4, where 3 is the numerator and 4 is the denominator. To add or subtract fractions, the denominators must first be the same; these are called like fractions. If the denominators are different (unlike fractions), find the lowest common denominator (LCD) by identifying the smallest number that both denominators divide into evenly, then convert each fraction to an equivalent fraction with that denominator before adding or subtracting the numerators. For example, to add 1/3 and 1/4, the LCD is 12, so 1/3 becomes 4/12 and 1/4 becomes 3/12, giving a sum of 7/12. When the numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator, the fraction is called an improper fraction and can be converted to a mixed number, such as 7/4 becoming 1 and 3/4. After adding or subtracting, fractions should always be simplified to their lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor. Word problems often describe sharing food, dividing distances, or combining measurements, and students must identify whether the fractions are like or unlike before choosing the correct method. Practising with number lines and fraction strips helps build an intuitive sense of fraction size before moving to purely numerical methods.
