Unit: The Water Cycle (Grade VI Science)

The water cycle, also called the hydrological cycle, describes how water continuously moves between the Earth's surface and the atmosphere. It begins with evaporation, where heat from the sun turns liquid water from oceans, rivers, and lakes into water vapour that rises into the air. Plants also release water vapour through a process called transpiration. As water vapour rises and cools at higher altitudes, it undergoes condensation, forming tiny droplets that cluster together to create clouds. When these droplets grow heavy enough, they fall back to the Earth's surface as precipitation, which can take the form of rain, snow, sleet, or hail depending on the temperature. Once precipitation reaches the ground, water can follow several paths: it may flow over the surface as runoff into rivers and eventually back into the ocean, soak into the soil through infiltration to replenish groundwater, or be absorbed by plant roots. Groundwater can slowly move through soil and rock layers before resurfacing in springs or being drawn up by wells. This continuous cycle is essential for distributing fresh water across the planet, supporting agriculture, drinking water supplies, and natural ecosystems. Students should be able to draw and label a diagram of the water cycle showing evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and collection, and explain why the cycle has no true beginning or end.
