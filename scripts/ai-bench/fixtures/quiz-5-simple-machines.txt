Unit: Simple Machines (Grade VI Science)

A simple machine is a basic mechanical device that changes the direction or magnitude of a force to make work easier. The six classical simple machines are the lever, wheel and axle, pulley, inclined plane, wedge, and screw. A lever is a rigid bar that pivots around a fixed point called the fulcrum, and it is classified into three classes depending on the relative positions of the fulcrum, the load, and the applied effort; a seesaw and a pair of scissors are common examples. A wheel and axle consists of a wheel attached to a central rod, allowing rotational motion to move objects with less effort, as seen in a doorknob or a bicycle. A pulley uses a grooved wheel and a rope or chain to lift loads, and combining multiple pulleys into a block-and-tackle system can greatly reduce the effort needed. An inclined plane is a flat, sloped surface, like a ramp, that allows a load to be raised using less force over a longer distance. A wedge is essentially two inclined planes joined back to back, used for splitting or cutting, such as an axe head. A screw is an inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder, converting rotational force into linear motion, as in a jar lid or a wood screw. Understanding mechanical advantage — the ratio of output force to input force — helps explain why these devices make everyday tasks easier.
