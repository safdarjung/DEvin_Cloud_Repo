Admissions team's feedback notes after the open-house day, compiled for the marketing committee's review.

Footfall at this year's open house was notably higher than last year, with over two hundred prospective families visiting across the single Saturday session, compared to roughly one hundred and fifty last year. The admissions desk recorded that the most frequently asked question concerned the school's fee structure and payment installment options, followed closely by questions about the student-to-teacher ratio in the primary section and the availability of transport on specific routes.

The campus tour groups, each led by a current student volunteer, received consistently positive feedback in the exit survey forms, with several parents specifically praising the confidence and articulate explanations given by the grade X volunteers about the science laboratories and library facilities. One recurring suggestion from parents was to extend the tour to include the sports field and the swimming pool area, which were visible only from a distance this year due to time constraints.

The information booth for the primary section saw longer queues than anticipated, and parents waiting more than fifteen minutes were offered refreshments by the hospitality volunteers while they waited; the admissions team recommends adding a second booth for the primary section next year given the growing interest in that age group specifically.

Several parents inquired about the school's approach to homework load and digital learning tools, indicating that these are becoming more important deciding factors compared to previous years when fee structure and location dominated the conversation. The marketing committee may want to prepare a short printed handout addressing these two questions directly for next year's open house, since staff fielding repeated verbal questions on the same two topics consumed a disproportionate amount of time.

Overall conversion from open-house attendance to actual application submissions will be tracked over the coming month, but initial informal counts at the admissions desk suggest interest levels are higher than the previous two years combined, which the committee should factor into seat planning discussions for the next academic year's intake.
