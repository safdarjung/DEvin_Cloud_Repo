Unit: Photosynthesis in Green Plants (Grade VII Science)

Plants are called producers because they can make their own food. The process by which green plants prepare food using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide is called photosynthesis. It takes place mainly in the leaves, inside structures called chloroplasts, which contain a green pigment called chlorophyll. Chlorophyll absorbs sunlight and uses its energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose, a simple sugar, and oxygen. The overall word equation is: carbon dioxide plus water, in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll, yields glucose plus oxygen. The oxygen produced is released into the atmosphere through tiny pores on the leaf surface called stomata, while the glucose is used by the plant for energy or stored as starch for later use. Photosynthesis usually happens during the day because it needs sunlight, and it slows down or stops at night. Factors that affect the rate of photosynthesis include light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, and temperature. Without photosynthesis, there would be no food chain and no oxygen for most living things to breathe, which is why plants are essential to almost every ecosystem on Earth. Students should be able to label a diagram of a leaf cross-section, write the balanced word equation, and explain why photosynthesis is important for life on Earth.
